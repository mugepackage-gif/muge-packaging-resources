## What buyers should confirm

### Product fit and weight

- Buyer inputs: Product size, item weight, set layout and whether the box is for retail display, ecommerce gifting or premium handoff.
- Why it matters: Rigid boxes are structure-led; unclear product fit creates sample revisions and weak quote comparisons.

### Opening structure

- Buyer inputs: Magnetic closure, drawer style, lift-off lid, book-style box, insert or tray direction and expected unboxing sequence.
- Why it matters: The opening style affects material planning, surface layout, insert design and sample review.

### Surface and finish

- Buyer inputs: Surface paper direction, logo placement, foil or spot effect reference, color coverage and texture expectation.
- Why it matters: Premium gift boxes are often judged visually before price comparison, so finish expectations must be explicit.

### Sampling purpose

- Buyer inputs: Whether the sample checks structure, color, insert fit, photography, retailer approval or internal sourcing review.
- Why it matters: A sample without a review purpose often delays approval instead of shortening the project path.

## RFQ checklist

- Product dimensions, item weight and set layout.
- Preferred rigid box structure or reference image.
- Insert or tray requirement and product-fit notes.
- Surface paper, logo placement, finish direction and color reference.
- Target market, sales channel and sample review purpose.
- Any document questions that need manual confirmation.
