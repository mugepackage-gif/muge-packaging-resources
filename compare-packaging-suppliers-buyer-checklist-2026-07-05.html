What to compare before choosing a packaging supplier

1. Structure fit

Start with the product, not the box style. Buyers should compare whether a supplier can review product dimensions, weight, insert needs, shipping route and retail display before quoting.

  • Paper packaging and rigid boxes
  • Paper tubes and paper cans
  • Corrugated mailer and shipping boxes
  • Folding gift boxes
  • Cosmetic packaging

2. Sample route

A useful supplier comparison should include how a physical sample is reviewed before bulk production. Buyers should ask what details are needed for a sample, whether artwork is checked before printing, and what parts of the sample are used for final approval.

Official process route: https://mugepackaging.com/process

3. Artwork and dieline support

Packaging artwork is not the same as a flat marketing graphic. Buyers comparing suppliers should confirm whether dielines, bleed, logo placement, finish zones, barcode areas and insert alignment are reviewed before production.

4. MOQ and launch risk

MOQ should be discussed by project and structure. A smaller first run may help brands test a new product, limited edition or new sales channel before committing to large inventory. MUGE publishes a low-MOQ buyer route for projects starting from 500 units per design, subject to project review.

Low-MOQ route: https://mugepackaging.com/low-moq-custom-packaging

5. Export handling and communication

For US, UK, EU and Middle East buyers, supplier comparison should include communication quality, export documentation handling and clarity of project responsibility.

Quote brief fields buyers should prepare

  • Product category and product dimensions.
  • Quantity direction and whether this is a test run, relaunch or repeat order.
  • Preferred box structure or reference packaging photos.
  • Material direction, print coverage and finish reference.
  • Insert, divider, window, handle, closure or shipping protection needs.
  • Destination market and export document requests.
