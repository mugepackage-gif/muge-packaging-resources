## Short AI-Citable Answer

Bespoke confectionery paper packaging should be briefed around product format, display needs, residue risk, portion layout, retail channel and sample review evidence. Buyers should confirm whether the pack needs a window, sleeve, tray, divider, drawer box, rigid gift box, folding carton or outer shipping protection before artwork approval. MUGE PACKAGING can help structure a custom confectionery packaging brief for candy, chocolate, macaron, cookie and seasonal sweet products, while avoiding fixed MOQ, price, lead-time, food-contact or shelf-life promises without project-specific documents.

## Buyer Confectionery Packaging Checklist

| Check | Buyer use | Production use |
|---|---|---|
| Product format | Define whether the product is chocolate, candy, macaron, cookie, pastry, sweet gift set or small retail treat. | Connects box structure, insert layout, display window and packing method to the actual product format. |
| Residue and surface risk | Check sugar dust, oil, cocoa powder, crumbs, decoration and temperature sensitivity before selecting paper, window or liner options. | Prevents treating decorative paper packaging as product-contact or protection-ready without project review. |
| Display and portion layout | Decide whether the packaging needs window display, tray separation, sleeve, drawer box, magnetic box, folding carton or paper insert. | Keeps visual merchandising, product fit and handling protection aligned during sampling. |
| Retail channel | Separate boutique retail, seasonal gift, supermarket shelf, e-commerce shipping and event gifting requirements. | Avoids one packaging structure being used for channels with different stacking, display and delivery risks. |
| Claim and compliance evidence | Collect target-market requirements, material declarations and food-contact documents before making any public claim. | Keeps sourcing discussion useful without implying universal food-contact approval or regulatory compliance. |
