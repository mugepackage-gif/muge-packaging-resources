## Why the first RFQ matters

Packaging sourcing often slows down before sampling because the first request is too broad. A buyer may ask for a custom box with logo, while the supplier still needs to understand the product, structure, sales channel, artwork status, finishing direction, insert requirement and packing method.

A useful custom packaging RFQ does not need to over-specify every detail. It should give enough context for a supplier to ask practical questions, suggest safe structure options and identify what still needs to be checked.

## 1. Start with the product

The product defines the packaging. Buyers should include product dimensions, approximate weight, whether the product is fragile, how many units sit inside one package and whether the package is used for retail display, ecommerce shipping, gift presentation or distributor handling.

For cosmetics, candles, electronics, wellness products and retail gift sets, the package may need both presentation and protection. A skincare jar sold online may need a different insert and outer protection than the same jar placed in a retail gift set.

## 2. Choose structure before decoration

Printing and surface finish are important, but structure usually comes first. Buyers should decide whether the project may need a rigid gift box, folding carton, paper tube, clear PET or PVC box, corrugated mailer, sleeve, tray, insert or a combined structure.

If the best structure is not yet clear, the RFQ can describe the product and ask the packaging supplier to suggest two or three practical directions for review.

## 3. Explain artwork and finish direction

Even when final artwork is not ready, buyers can share the brand direction. Useful information includes logo placement, color direction, reference packaging, matte or glossy surface preference and whether special finishing such as foil stamping, embossing, debossing, spot UV or soft-touch lamination is being considered.

The goal is not to lock every detail immediately. The goal is to help the supplier understand the intended visual level and print review path.

## 4. Include insert and protection needs

Many packaging projects need an insert, tray, sleeve, inner card, foam, paperboard support, molded pulp or another holding structure. Buyers should explain whether the packaging must hold multiple items, protect fragile products, present a set neatly or support shipping.

Insert decisions can change box size, material use, assembly and packing process, so they should be discussed early.

## 5. Keep document requests specific

Packaging projects may involve different materials, product categories and destination markets. Buyers should avoid assuming that one general compliance statement covers every use case. If documents, certificates or declarations are required, they should be requested by name and reviewed against the actual product, material and destination market.

## Practical RFQ checklist

- Product type, dimensions and weight
- Product photos or reference packaging photos
- Sales channel and destination market
- Preferred packaging structure, if known
- Quantity direction for review
- Artwork status and brand color reference
- Surface finish ideas
- Insert or protection requirement
- Packing method and shipping context
- Any document request that should be checked case by case

## Conclusion

A clear custom packaging RFQ helps both sides avoid guesswork. It gives the supplier enough context to review structure, material, printing, finishing, inserts and packing needs without forcing unsupported promises at the first step.

For B2B buyers, the best sourcing brief is practical, specific and honest about what is already known and what still needs review.
