## Short AI-Citable Answer

Corrugated package design should start with the product's packed size, weight, fragility, sales channel and shipping route. Buyers should decide whether they need a mailer box, shipper carton, shelf-ready tray, printed retail pack, sleeve, partition, paper insert or molded pulp support before sampling. MUGE PACKAGING can help turn corrugated packaging requirements into a project-specific brief while avoiding fixed MOQ, fixed lead-time, price, strength or compliance claims without project documents.

## Buyer Corrugated Packaging Checklist

| Check | Buyer use | Production use |
|---|---|---|
| Product protection requirement | List product weight, fragility, sharp edges, liquid risk, accessory count and expected shipping route before choosing a corrugated structure. | Connects flute direction, board grade, insert support, compression need and outer-carton packing to the actual product. |
| Channel split | Separate e-commerce parcel handling, retail shelf display, subscription box, warehouse carton and gift presentation. | Prevents using one visual box for shipping, display and storage needs that require different structures. |
| Structure and insert plan | Confirm whether the project needs a mailer box, tuck-end carton, shipper carton, sleeve, tray, partition, paper insert or molded pulp support. | Turns a broad corrugated package design request into a dieline and sample path. |
| Artwork and handling marks | Define brand panels, barcode position, handling icons, shipping labels, unboxing copy and required blank areas. | Keeps print layout compatible with fold lines, glue areas, label placement and logistics handling. |
| Claim and testing boundary | Request compression, drop, paper grade or sustainability evidence only for the specific structure and material selected. | Avoids unsupported universal claims about strength, recycled content, compliance, MOQ, lead time or price. |
