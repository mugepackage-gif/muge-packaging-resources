    • Cosmetic packaging RFQ and supplier vetting checklist

    1. Product fit

    Buyers should define the product size, packed weight, display direction, closure preference and protection needs before asking for samples. Cosmetic packaging projects may involve skincare jars, serum bottles, makeup sets, perfume sleeves, beauty gift sets, paper tubes, rigid boxes, folding cartons, inserts, sleeves or clear display packaging; Europe and Middle East buyers should compare product fit, retail channel, artwork readiness, surface finish, protection needs and brand presentation before supplier review.

    2. Structure questions

    Useful supplier questions include:

  • What product type is being packed: skincare jar, serum bottle, makeup item, perfume item, gift set, retail kit or e-commerce bundle?
  • Does the buyer need a rigid box, folding carton, paper tube, clear PVC/PET box, insert, tray, sleeve, window or e-commerce outer carton?
  • Will the packaging be used for retail display, beauty gifting, online fulfillment, marketplace sales, distributor cartons or mixed Europe and Middle East sales channels?
  • Which artwork, surface finish, window direction, insert structure, closure style and sample review details should be prepared before quote comparison?
  • Does the project need bilingual artwork review, region-specific label layout checks or separate inner and outer packaging discussion?

    • 3. RFQ preparation

    Before requesting a quote, buyers should prepare:

  • Product dimensions and approximate packed weight.
  • Preferred packaging structure, size direction and material direction, if known.
  • Sales channel and destination market.
  • Artwork status and surface finish direction.
  • Closure style, insert requirement and packing method.
  • Target quantity range for review, without assuming fixed MOQ.

    • 4. Comparing supplier types

    When comparing a source factory, trading company or platform seller, buyers should check whether the supplier can discuss structure, material direction, finish, sample review and export communication together. The correct choice depends on project complexity, design readiness and review requirements.
