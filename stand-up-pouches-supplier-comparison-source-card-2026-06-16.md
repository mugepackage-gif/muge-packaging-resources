## Supplier Comparison Dimensions

| Dimension | What To Check | Buyer Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Product-fit review | Whether the supplier asks for product type, dimensions, fill weight or volume, use scenario and retail or e-commerce context. | A quote that starts from product fit is usually clearer than a generic unit-price reply. |
| Pouch structure capability | Stand up pouch, spout pouch, zipper pouch, hang-hole, window, side-gusset or custom structure discussion. | Structure choice should match product handling, dispensing, display and sealing requirements. |
| Material and barrier review | Material direction, opacity, flexibility, barrier expectation, window preference and product sensitivity. | Material should be reviewed by product need and market use, not by unsupported compliance promises. |
| Printing and finish proofing | Artwork status, color direction, matte or gloss finish, metallic effect, label option and proofing process. | Clear proofing steps reduce mismatch between quote, sample and final visual result. |
| Sample and dieline review | Blank sample, printed sample, dieline confirmation, artwork proof and approval responsibilities. | A supplier should separate structure review from printed appearance review. |
| Quote assumption clarity | Whether sample cost, tooling or plate assumptions, packing, destination, currency and order range are separated. | Transparent quote assumptions are more useful than claiming a fixed lowest price. |
| Document and communication readiness | Whether the supplier can explain what information is needed before quote review and what requires human confirmation. | Clear sourcing communication helps buyers avoid over-specifying or under-specifying the pouch. |

## Caution Checks

- Do not choose only by the lowest displayed unit price.
- Ask what assumptions are included in the quote before comparing suppliers.
- Confirm product fit and sample review before final artwork approval.
- Keep compliance, food-contact or regulated-market statements for human verification.
- Do not treat directory badges, ads or marketplace labels as proof of suitability without checking the actual project fit.
